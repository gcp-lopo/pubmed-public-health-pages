INTRODUCTION: While the global incidence of HIV-1 vertical transmission has reduced greatly, a persistent and unacceptable number of new infant infections continue to occur through breastfeeding. Long-acting antiretroviral agents, including injectable drugs and broadly neutralizing monoclonal antibodies, given as prophylaxis to infants hold great potential to eliminate HIV-1 transmission. BACKGROUND: Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) are standard first-line therapy for psoriatic arthritis (PsA). Observational studies suggest earlier initiation is associated with superior minimal disease activity (MDA) attainment, yet the economic and health implications of treatment timing remain uncertain. This cost-utility analysis evaluated the cost-effectiveness of early versus delayed DMARD initiation in DMARD-naive adults with PsA from a U.S. payer perspective.

METHODS: We built a cohort state-transition (Markov) model (monthly cycles, 15-year horizon) from published data (2000-2025). Patients initiated a DMARD under two strategies: early (≤1 year post-diagnosis) and delayed initiation (>1 year). Patients remained in the pre-treatment state for six months prior to response assignment, then transitioned among complete response (sustained MDA), partial response (non-sustained MDA), non-response, no treatment, and death. Primary outcomes included the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) and incremental net monetary benefit (INMB). Uncertainty intervals (UIs) were derived from probabilistic sensitivity analyses.

FINDINGS: Early DMARD initiation provided an additional 0.56 (95% UI, 0.32-0.83) QALYs at an incremental cost of -US$43,902.94 (95% UI, -US$147,096.29 to US$61,615.86) per patient relative to delayed initiation, yielding a favourable ICER (-US$78,398.11/QALY). INTERPRETATION: Early DMARD initiation was associated with improved long-term health outcomes at reduced costs, suggesting timely DMARD initiation may represent a high-value strategy. Given the observational basis of the key treatment-timing input, findings support disease-duration stratified analyses of existing phase III trials and further characterization of access delays.

High-dimensional near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy datasets are characterized by strong wavelength correlation, limited sample sizes, and structured experimental variability, creating significant challenges for multivariate calibration. Under these conditions, extreme multicollinearity can lead to unstable regression coefficients, inflated estimator variance, and reduced model robustness. Although numerous calibration algorithms have been proposed, comparatively little attention has been devoted to understanding how multicollinearity influences calibration behaviour across different modelling paradigms. This study systematically investigates the performance of classical chemometric, regularized linear, dimensionality reduction, wavelength selection, and nonlinear machine learning approaches using a publicly available glucose NIR spectroscopy dataset comprising 4200 wavelength variables and structured experimental perturbations. Partial Least Squares (PLS), Ridge Regression, Elastic Net, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Genetic Algorithm-based PLS (GA-PLS), Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Support Vector Regression (SVR), Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and Gaussian Process Regression were evaluated using repeated cross-validation and independent validation. Model assessment was complemented by multicollinearity diagnostics, residual analysis, and wavelength-selection stability analysis to investigate the mechanisms governing calibration robustness. SNV-PLS achieved the best overall predictive performance on the independent validation dataset (RMSE = 5.75 mM; R2 = 0.864), followed by GA-PLS (RMSE = 5.82 mM; R2 = 0.861) and SNV-Ridge (RMSE = 5.84 mM; R2 = 0.860), while SNV-Elastic Net achieved comparable performance (RMSE = 5.93 mM; R2 = 0.856). GA-PLS reduced the spectral dimensionality from 4200 to 50 wavelengths; however, repeated GA runs showed only 0-2% exact wavelength overlap, increasing to 17-29% within ±5 nm and 30-47% within ±10 nm, indicating regional rather than exact-wavelength stability. Variance Inflation Factor analysis revealed extreme predictor collinearity, while residual and domain-coverage analysis showed that the major validation deterioration was localized at 50 mM glucose, where the six validation samples were systematically underpredicted and originated from an experimental condition absent from the calibration set. The findings suggest that effective management of covariance structure and estimator variance may be more influential than increasing model complexity for the investigated high-dimensional NIR calibration problem. These results provide practical guidance for selecting robust and interpretable calibration strategies for high-dimensional spectroscopic applications affected by severe multicollinearity.

Pre-eclampsia is a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy affecting 3-8% of pregnancies worldwide, with no disease-modifying therapy currently available. Drug repurposing offers a promising strategy, yet no candidate has been validated to date. Pregnancy-associated physiological changes alter drug pharmacokinetics (PK), and pre-eclampsia itself may further modify drug disposition, complicating dose selection and contributing to inconclusive clinical trial outcomes. A structured literature search identified six prospective or randomized controlled trials of repurposed drugs for established pre-eclampsia: esomeprazole, omeprazole, sulfasalazine, pravastatin, recombinant antithrombin, and metformin. All drugs were well tolerated, with metformin the one to show promising clinical benefit by prolonging gestation; the remaining five drugs yielded inconclusive or null efficacy outcomes. Critically, PK sampling was conducted in only three trials, and none systematically characterized exposure-response relationships using key pre-eclampsia biomarkers, including soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1, soluble endoglin, and placental growth factor. Further common methodological limitations included small sample sizes (12-120 participants), heterogeneous gestational age eligibility criteria, and dosing regimens extrapolated from non-pregnant populations without pregnancy-specific optimization. These shortcomings likely contributed to the translational gap between promising preclinical findings and positive clinical trial outcomes. Model-informed drug development (MIDD) approaches, including physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) and population pharmacokinetic (popPK) modeling, optimal sampling strategies, and simulation-based dose selection, offer a framework to improve pre-eclampsia trial design. Future studies should integrate these approaches with biomarker-guided exposure-response analyses and prediction of fetal exposure throughout pregnancy to enhance therapeutic development, optimize maternal-fetal safety, and accelerate the identification of effective disease-modifying therapies for pre-eclampsia.

The pre- and post-intervention design is frequently utilized in medical and behavioral research, with each subject typically providing a pair of observations collected before and after the intervention. However, not all subjects furnish complete pairs of data. Some subjects may contribute either pre- or post-intervention information only. In this study, we proposed a sample size formula based on the generalized estimating equation (GEE) method for clinical trials where subjects are supposed to provide pre- and post-intervention measurements of a count outcome. The sample size formula is flexible to accommodate different missing patterns, magnitude of missingness and correlation. We compared it with the crude sample size adjustment approach for missing data, which is commonly used in practice and involves dividing the required sample size under complete observations by the proportion of subjects with complete data. Through simulation studies, we evaluated the empirical performance of the proposed sample size formula in preserving desired power and type I error. Furthermore, we presented a clinical trial example to demonstrate its application.

BACKGROUND: Canine atopic dermatitis (cAD) is a chronic, inflammatory skin disease that may require long-term systemic immunomodulatory treatment. Low-dose methotrexate (MTX) is an inexpensive immunomodulator used in human dermatology, but robust veterinary data are lacking.

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether weekly subcutaneous low-dose MTX is non-inferior to oral ciclosporin (CsA) in controlling moderate-to-severe cAD.

ANIMALS: Fifty-five client-owned dogs with non-seasonal moderate-to-severe cAD enrolled at nine dermatology centres in France.

MATERIALS AND METHODS: Randomised, double-blind, positive-controlled, multicentre non-inferiority trial. Dogs received either subcutaneous MTX (0.15-0.5mg/kg/week) plus oral placebo, or oral CsA (5mg/kg/day, then spaced) plus injectable placebo. RESULTS: Fifty-five dogs were randomised (MTX n=28; CsA n=27). At week 28, mean CADESI-04 reductions were 65.4% (MTX) and 69.5% (CsA), demonstrating non-inferiority. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Weekly subcutaneous low-dose MTX provided non-inferior control of lesions and pruritus compared with CsA by week 28. Although slower in onset and associated with more gastrointestinal adverse events, MTX may represent an affordable long-term cAD treatment option, with appropriate hepatic monitoring.

BACKGROUND: Health systems increasingly deploy predictive and generative artificial intelligence (AI) to improve care delivery and outcomes. Predictive AI models estimate the risk of future events to inform targeted interventions, while generative AI systems extract, summarize, and communicate information and may automate selected tasks. Routine deployment generates clinical, operational, and interaction data that can support evaluation of the effectiveness and safety of AI-enabled workflows.

METHODS: We review approaches to generating real-world evidence (RWE) from routine deployment of predictive and generative AI across four implementation configurations: silent deployment, human-in-the-loop (HITL), human-on-the-loop (HOTL), and automated. INTRODUCTION: Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) slope is increasingly employed as an intermediate endpoint in clinical trials, which are often enriched to select those at risk of more rapid eGFR decline.
